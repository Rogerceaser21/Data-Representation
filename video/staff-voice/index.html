The proposal in one page

From observation to a plan for every teacher

The R3 pipeline already takes a lesson observation and turns it into a referenced coaching plan for the teacher. This page sets out the two additions Steve asked for, an evaluation of the professional learning session and a reflection with an action plan, and shows where each one sits in the flow that is already running.

The loop today

Four steps, already running

01
Observation filed
R3 form, on the inspector's iPad, in the lesson.
02
Teacher given feedback
The conversation that follows the lesson.
03
Observer approves the plan
An analysis and a suggested coaching plan, which the observer approves, edits or rewrites.
04
Teacher receives it
Every suggestion referenced back to the AIS Student Progress Rubric.

Every claim the board makes carries a numbered marker. Opening it shows the lessons behind the claim, the inspector's own words, and a link to the locked record. More than six hundred of those references sit behind the June round. References never name a teacher.

What makes it usable

Five strategies, mapped to the rubric

Five coaching strategies with worked examples, sitting against the Student Progress Rubric point by point and built on the AIS Teaching and Learning Agenda. The observer and the analysis draw from the same shelf, so the plan the teacher receives is recognisably ours, and drafting it takes the observer minutes rather than an evening.

Rubric point
·Students know what success looks like before they begin
·Questioning checks understanding rather than moves the lesson on
·Work is adapted so every group is stretched
·Students talk to each other about the learning
Strategy offered
Success criteria written with the class, kept visible
Cold call plus a follow-up "how do you know?"
One task, three entry points
Structured talk: pair, compare, then report
Exit ticket that names the next lesson's starting point
Who sees what

By role, not one report for everyone

Leadership

Themes across the school rather than a folder of individual replies. Where support is being asked for, which challenges repeat and where, each claim carrying its references.

Middle leaders

Their own team and nobody else's. Not a report to read through, a starting point for a coaching conversation with the teacher's own words already in front of them.

The teacher

A plan of their own, built from their own lesson and their own words, in language the school already uses. The same thing the observation gives them now.
