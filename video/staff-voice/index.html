Questioning is the lever here. The evidence shows understanding being checked with the students who volunteer, and the lesson moving on before the rest have answered. One change to the questioning routine moves this from Acceptable to Good.

Questioning

Follow every answer with one question that asks the student to justify it, before you move on.

Look for
Students explaining their reasoning without being asked twice, including those who did not volunteer.

Cold call, then "how do you know?". Name the student, pause, then ask the follow-up, so the whole class stays with the thinking rather than waiting for the answer.

New on the shelf

Claire Gadsby Teacher Strategies

The strategy shelf now carries named Claire Gadsby Teacher Strategies, one for every point of the rubric, drawn from Dynamically Different Classrooms by Claire Gadsby and Jan Evans and rewritten as AIS practice for our own rooms and kit. Where the evidence names a gap, the plan recommends the matching strategy with its concrete setup. Two examples:

Class Response Boards

Trigger: questioning reaches only the volunteers.

1One wipe clean board and pen on every desk, within reach.
2Every explanation ends with boards up.
3Scan, say what you saw, then move on or reteach on the spot.
4Collect nothing.

The teacher says: "Boards up on three. I am reading the room, not marking you." Every answer visible in eight seconds, so a concept is retaught on the spot, not next week.

The Pupil Corner

Trigger: student engagement, ownership of the unit.

1Mark one area. A metre square is enough.
2Students add something related to the unit once a week: an artefact, a clipping, a question they cannot answer yet.
3The teacher reviews it with the students at the start of a lesson.
4The teaching that follows builds on the current student need.

The teacher says: "What's new in our corner this week?"

After Claire Gadsby and Jan Evans, Dynamically Different Classrooms (Independent Thinking Press). These are AIS setups for our rooms; staff wanting the original techniques should read the book.

Designed for one thing

A concrete plan of action

All of it is designed for one thing: giving every teacher a concrete plan of action. Drawing on any of the Coaching Strategies and their worked examples, the plan targets the band above, so teachers keep moving up the judgement scale, becoming better teachers, and students make better progress in every lesson.
