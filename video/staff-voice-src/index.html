Facilitating better progressGood→Very Good

Questioning is the lever here. The evidence shows understanding being checked with the students who volunteer, and the lesson moving on before the rest have answered. One change to the questioning routine moves this from Acceptable to Good.

Questioning

Follow every answer with one question that asks the student to justify it, before you move on.

Look for
Students explaining their reasoning without being asked twice, including those who did not volunteer.

Cold call, then "how do you know?". Name the student, pause, then ask the follow-up, so the whole class stays with the thinking rather than waiting for the answer.

Technique source: Tips for Teachers →

Grounded in

In the AIS OTP Progress Rubric, to facilitate better than expected progress at a Good level, the teacher checks understanding through targeted questioning and adapts the lesson in response. View the rubric →

Class Response Boards

Coaching plan · Teacher Strategy
R3 June 26 Recommended Trigger · questioning reached volunteers only
Class Response Boards
  1. 1One wipe clean board and pen on every desk, within reach.
  2. 2Every explanation ends with boards up.
  3. 3Scan, say what you saw, then move on or reteach on the spot.
  4. 4Collect nothing.
The teacher says

"Boards up on three. I am reading the room, not marking you."

Pupil Owned Corner

Teacher Strategy · student engagement
Ownership of the unit One square metre · a rotating pair · a fortnight
The Pupil Owned Corner
  1. 1

    Mark one area. A metre square is enough.

  2. 2

    A rotating pair curates it for a fortnight, linked to the unit: an artefact, a clipping, a question they cannot answer.

  3. 3

    Sixty seconds at the start of one lesson.

  4. 4

    The next five minutes of teaching build on what they said.

After Claire Gadsby and Jan Evans, Dynamically Different Classrooms
