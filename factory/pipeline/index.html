Where the factory stops

The four gates

Four moments need your answer. Everything else runs on its own.

Gate 1

The words

When
After the script is written, before any voice exists.
What I decide
The exact words the video will say out loud.
Say this
"Yes, those are the words, generate the take."
"Scene 4 line 2 should say this instead: ..."
Costs me
2 minutes to answer. Changing your mind later means a full retake: cents plus about an hour of machine time.

Gate 2

The pace

When
After the recording is cut into scenes.
What I decide
A slightly rushed delivery, or a longer video. You get both as audio you can play.
Say this
"Natural pace at 3m15, ship that one."
"Take the 10 percent faster cut at 3m00."
Costs me
2 minutes to answer, most of it listening. Changing later costs about an hour of machine time, but no new recording.

When
After the video renders, before the final mix.

The mood of the whole video, not just one track.
Say this

Costs me
2 minutes to answer. Picking from your library costs nothing. A fresh batch costs about 72 cents and 15 minutes.

Same visuals, new words.

  2. Approve the new wording at Gate 1.

Step by step

The 11 automatic steps (open only if curious)

Nothing here needs you, except at the four gates. Tap a step to open it.

0 Setupcopy a video that already works No gate

What happens

  • Claude asks the brief once: topic, audience, what it shows, where it ships, page or no page.
  • It copies the last known good video into a temporary folder and deletes the old voice track.
  • The look comes from the brand file. The visuals are built from the real product's own styling.

Tools that run

  • cp -R sample-staff-voice/staff-voice-src copies the known good video
  • cp template/scripts/score_take.py scripts/ adds the voice scorer
  • rm -f assets/narration.* deletes the old voice files

What can go wrong

  • Building inside Google Drive. Drive undoes edits, so work happens in a temporary folder.
  • Forgetting the scorer. Then step 4 has nothing to run.

Gate

None. Runs on its own.

1 Scriptthe words, plus real numbers Gate 1

What happens

  • Claude writes the narration, one block per scene, with a note on how to read it.
  • Roughly 550 words is three minutes.
  • Every number on screen is calculated by the product's own code. Anything else is labelled proposed on screen.
  • Teacher names are always invented.

Tools that run

  • scenes.json the script file
  • git show origin/main:... pulls the product's current code

What can go wrong

  • Reading the product code from an old copy. The everyday copy is months out of date.
  • Words that flip meaning with stress (present, live, read, record, project). A wrong reading costs the whole recording.
  • Long dashes in on-screen text. House style forbids them. Ordinary hyphens are fine.

Gate

Gate 1: you approve the exact wording before any audio exists. Say yes, or name the lines to change and what they should say. Two minutes.

2 Takeone request, one recording No gate

What happens

  • The whole approved script goes to the voice engine in one single request.
  • One complete recording of the whole script is called a take. It is accepted or binned as a whole.
  • Sending it whole is what keeps the voice sounding like one person.
  • The script measures the speaking speed and asks again if it is off, up to five attempts.
  • About a minute and a few cents.

Tools that run

  • node scripts/tts-single.mjs records the whole script in one pass

What can go wrong

  • The engine cuts off mid-script or races. The script spots both and rejects the take.
  • Recording line by line, or stretching single lines. Both are banned. That is the two-narrators bug.
  • Reopening the voice engine question. A local engine was tried on 2026-08-03 and lost.

Gate

None. Runs on its own.

3 Cut and pacesplit it, tighten it, even it out Gate 2

What happens

  • The one long recording is cut into scenes at its longest silences.
  • The pace check reads the speed of EVERY scene, not the average. The average hides the AI voice's habit of starting calm and racing toward the end.
  • A safety brake slows any scene that reads faster than the video's own opening scenes. The brake calibrates itself to each new recording. It can never speed a scene up.
  • A second pass trims dead air inside scenes. That takes about 28 seconds off four minutes.
  • A third pass evens out slow tone drift. It runs exactly once.

Tools that run

  • node scripts/split.mjs cuts scenes and applies the speed cap
  • python3 scripts/tighten.py shortens the pauses
  • python3 scripts/deflutter.py evens out tone

What can go wrong

  • Hand-editing the timing files. The scripts regenerate them, so the edits vanish or break sync.
  • Running the tone pass twice, which applies the correction twice.
  • Removing the per-scene speed cap. That brings back the narrator who speeds up and falls off.

Gate

Gate 2: pace against length is your call. You get both cuts as audio with real durations. Pick one. Two minutes.

4 Score the voicenumbers decide, not opinion Automatic check

What happens

  • Two scripts measure how different each scene sounds from the one next to it.
  • That gets compared against the natural wobble inside a single scene.
  • Smaller than the wobble means the take is one person, so it is kept.
  • Bigger means the whole take is binned and remade from step 2. One minute, one call.

Tools that run

  • python3 scripts/voiceconsist.py measures scene to scene difference
  • python3 scripts/score_take.py prints one line of pass numbers

What can go wrong

  • Shipping a take nobody measured. Banned. That jump got two versions rejected by ear.
  • Assuming the tone pass fixed it. On v1.3 it had not.
  • Skipping the check because the last take was fine.

Gate

No gate for you. An automatic check that must pass before anything moves on.

5 Composepictures pinned to the words No gate

What happens

  • The visuals are written into a template, then generated into the finished file.
  • Every animation is pinned to the voice timings, so pictures land on the words.
  • Late animations are pinned to a share of the line, so a shorter line squeezes instead of losing its end.
  • Highlights are rationed to five per three-minute video. The video ends on a silent eight-second card.

Tools that run

  • node scripts/build.mjs generates the finished file
  • npm run check automatic pre-render inspection

What can go wrong

  • Editing the generated file instead of the template. The next build overwrites it.
  • Adding the end card without re-baking the audio to the new length, so the card plays silent.
  • Motion that leaves things hidden. The resting picture has to be the finished picture.

Gate

None. Runs on its own.

6 Render and verifyframes pulled, sound measured Automatic check

What happens

  • The video renders to a finished file. That takes about as long as the video runs.
  • Still frames are pulled at the cover, at each highlight and at the end card, and looked at.
  • Frames are also pulled from the middle of animations, because a picture can look right at rest and blink in the middle.
  • Loudness is measured, not assumed.

You do not see the video here unless you asked for it. To get a viewing, say so at the plan stage.

Tools that run

  • npm run render renders the finished video
  • ffmpeg -frames:v 1 pulls single still frames
  • ffmpeg -af volumedetect measures loudness

What can go wrong

  • Waiting for a completion message that never prints. The new file appearing is the real signal.
  • Claiming it is correct without pulling a frame. A green build is not proof.
  • Checking only the settled moments.

Gate

No gate for you. An automatic check: pulled frames and measured sound, or it does not pass.

7 Musicyour library first, new tracks second Gate 3

What happens

  • Your music library is checked first. cine1, drive3 and tech3 are paid for and approved, so they skip straight to the mix.
  • New tracks are only made for a mood the library does not cover, at about eight cents a track.
  • Every candidate must have real movement, a steady body, no sung words, and be longer than the video.
  • Survivors are mixed under the same 30 seconds of real narration, so you compare like with like.

Tools that run

  • template/scripts/lyria-sweep.py generates new tracks to brief
  • knowledge/music-register-briefs.json the three written mood briefs
  • ffmpeg loudnorm and amix mixes music under real narration

What can go wrong

  • Going back to free libraries. Two rounds were rejected outright.
  • Picking by ear before measuring. Flat pads are the "makes me fall asleep" verdict.
  • Mixing the music so quietly it cannot be heard at all.

Gate

Gate 3: you name the track. Say "use cine1" and the music round is skipped entirely. Otherwise you get three previews and pick one.

After the pick, the track is filtered, levelled and ducked under the voice, with the fades baked to the exact video length. What you preview is what renders.

8 Companion pagevideo first, sticky while playing No gate

What happens

  • If the video gets a web page, the page is built video first: masthead, short lead-in, video, then the headline and the article.
  • The video sticks to the top of the screen while it plays and releases when you pause.
  • The page ships the video, a separate subtitle file, a fresh cover image and the logo.

Tools that run

  • knowledge/page-patterns.md the layout patterns
  • python3 -m http.server 8765 serves the page locally for real testing
  • getBoundingClientRect measures real positions while scrolling

What can go wrong

  • Opening the page as a file instead of through a local server. Nothing runs that way, so it looks broken.
  • Leaving the video nested inside another box, which silently kills the sticky behaviour.
  • Trusting screenshots. They can be stale, so positions are measured in the page.

Gate

None. Runs on its own.

9 Shipfingerprint, push, then check it live Gate 4

What happens

  • Every file is fingerprinted on both sides before it is committed, so nothing Drive altered slips through.
  • The video and the page go out as one commit with a version tag.
  • After publishing, the live video is downloaded back and fingerprinted against the local copy.
  • The live page is checked for a marker unique to this release.

Tools that run

  • md5 fingerprints each file on both sides
  • git push origin HEAD:main --tags publishes video and page together
  • curl plus md5 proves the live copy matches

What can go wrong

  • Calling it done before the live check. Done means live, verified, and the exact link in your hand.
  • Splitting the video and the page across two commits.

Gate

Gate 4: you decided this at the plan stage. If the approved plan ends live, it ships without another ask. By default nobody shows you the video before the public sees it.

10 Closeoutnotes and lessons written back No gate

What happens

  • The task list is brought up to date. Only provably finished work gets closed.
  • Project notes, the readme, the memory file and a handoff for the next session are refreshed.
  • Anything learned the hard way is written into the factory files, so the next video starts ahead.

Tools that run

  • Focus OS task update closes finished tasks
  • graphify update . rebuilds the project map
  • knowledge/gotchas.md where new traps get recorded

What can go wrong

  • Closing tasks that are not verifiably done.
  • Letting a hard-won lesson die in the session, which means paying for it again.

Gate

None. Runs on its own.

Non negotiable

The nine rules

Each one was earned by something going wrong.

  1. One recording, made in one go, measured before it is used.
  2. Build the video from the real product's own styling.
  3. Every number on screen comes from the product's own code.
  4. Never edit the generated file. Edit the template and rebuild.
  5. Build outside Google Drive. Copy in with a fingerprint check.
  6. Music ships pre-mixed, ducked under the voice, fades baked in.
  7. Igor signs off words, pace, music, and anything going outside.
  8. No school logos and no school names in outward facing videos.
  9. Reword stress-ambiguous words before recording. One bad reading costs everything.

The words used here

Glossary

Ten terms, one line each.

take = one complete recording of the whole script, made in one request.

gate = a moment the factory stops and waits for your answer.

mood family (register) = the character of a music track: cinematic, corporate drive, tech pulse.

ducking = the music dips while the narrator speaks and rises again in the gaps.

hold = a deliberate silent gap in the narration so the music can be heard alone.

pace cap = the safety net that slows any scene reading too fast, never speeds one up.

cue = the instruction that fires an on-screen animation at a chosen moment.

end card = the silent card held after the last spoken word.

companion page = the web page that carries the video plus the same argument in writing.

sweep = one round of generating nine new music tracks to brief, about 72 cents.
